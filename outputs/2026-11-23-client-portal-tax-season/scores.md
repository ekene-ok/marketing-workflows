## Reasons (one line per score that is not a 4)
- **A5-v1:** Clarity 5, one clear action with the offer stated. CTA 5, the headline is the cross-sell CTA from `messaging.md`. Brand voice 5, plain and calm
- **A1-v1:** ICP fit 5, uses "tax season" and "chasing" from the ICP. Proof 5, approved 4-days-faster stat. CTA 3, relies on the ad button for the action
- **A5-v2:** ICP fit 5, answers the "no time to switch tools" objection directly. Proof 3, the offer is concrete but there is no outcome proof
- **A2-v2:** Proof 5, approved stat tied to billable time. CTA 3, no explicit next step
- **A2-v1:** ICP fit 5, "time your team cannot bill" is the owner's language. Proof 2, no supporting evidence
- **A4-v1:** Brand voice 3, "are a risk" leans on worry more than our calm voice prefers
- **A1-v2:** Clarity 3, two ideas (reminders and security) compete in one short ad
- **A3-v1:** Proof 2, no evidence that clients prefer it
- **A4-v2:** ICP fit 3, security is a secondary concern for most of this audience; CTA 2, no next step
- **A3-v2:** Proof 1 and Clarity 3, "look as organised as you are" is vague and unsupported. Do not ship

## Recommended for testing
1. **A5-v1** (offer-led): highest score; tests whether the free-until-April offer alone moves customers
2. **A1-v1** (pain-led): strongest use of ICP language and proof; tests the "stop chasing" message
3. **A2-v2** (capacity-led): chosen over A5-v2 so all three test a different angle. A win here means owners respond to billable time more than to convenience

Running three different angles teaches which motivation works, not just which wording.
